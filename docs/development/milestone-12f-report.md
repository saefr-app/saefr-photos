## Astra review blockers and corrective verification

The independent Astra review reproduced two contract violations with real localhost HTTP and disposable PostgreSQL: inherited `synchronous_commit=off` allowed completed deletion and synthetic removal without a durable WAL acknowledgement; multipart Trash and Restore requests returned 200 and changed lifecycle versions despite the empty-body contract. Neither probe touched development data or real media. These findings supersede the initial M12F assessment of no production-code defect.

Irreversible acceptance now executes `SET LOCAL synchronous_commit TO on` at the start of its actual Kysely transaction. Journal completion executes the same setting on its own transaction before the completed-state update. An inability to establish the setting rolls back acceptance or leaves completion pending. No global PostgreSQL setting or pool default changes. The short claim update, renewal and failure recording retain their existing repeat-safe token protocol: a lost claim acknowledgement requires positive token confirmation, every subsequent I/O step rechecks the claim, and loss of a claim cannot erase the durable acceptance journal. Claim acknowledgement itself does not establish irreversible deletion authority; a database crash can lose that short lease, leaving pending intent for explicit retry against the same immutable targets. No database connection is held during filesystem I/O.

Trash and Restore now share the early framed-body guard already used for permanent DELETE and retry. It runs after authentication, Origin and CSRF, before Fastify parsing; it rejects positive Content-Length and any Transfer-Encoding without buffering multipart data. Both controllers also invoke the shared body predicate. Genuinely empty and explicit zero-length requests retain their success/ETag behavior. Upload parsing and other asset routes are unchanged.

## Data safety, recovery and compatibility

Only a committed, owner-scoped journal authorizes removal of exact original/thumbnail targets under a verified two-role storage binding. Acceptance retires the asset UUID in its transaction; uploaded IDs are checked against the retired journal. Short execution tokens prevent stale claimants from recording success, and absence of both files plus required persistence acknowledgement precedes completion. A request interruption or process crash may leave a pending receipt. M12 provides explicit owner retry and status, but no background liveness guarantee. An already-issued provider call may finish after disconnect; the executor retains its local slot until it drains and starts no subsequent call when cancellation is observed.

The historical M8/M10/M11 migration files and inspectors were not changed in M12F. Startup checks supported exact history and never migrates, provisions storage or launches recovery. M11 compatibility is limited to its approved historical schema boundary; older strict binaries cannot serve M12 schema. Legacy NULL-owned assets remain inaccessible. There was no access to the development PostgreSQL database, existing photographs, named Docker volumes or real storage-adoption operations.

## Outstanding gates and risks

- Fresh hosted native ext4 certification for the corrected implementation, separately reviewed repository-layout checkpoint and renewed relevant certification remain before any M12 merge decision. PR #9 stays draft.
- No backup/restore rehearsal, real-data migration or storage adoption has been approved. Deployment TLS/proxy, operator fencing and first-alpha recovery procedures remain unverified.
- The supported initial profile is the specifically observed Linux/ext4 arrangement. Windows, macOS, NAS, other mount profiles, reboot and hardware power-loss behavior are not certified. Native ENOSPC and full-disk conditions are not represented by an actual full-disk fixture.
- A database/filesystem crash can leave pending deletion or upload residue. M13 must reuse this journal/executor for automatic retries, Trash expiry and report-first maintenance; none runs in M12.
- Already-open streams may finish after revocation. Cursor pagination does not provide a mobile synchronization change feed or a snapshot under concurrent mutation.
- Dependency-audit findings previously reported in the phase reports remain untriaged; this phase made no dependency changes.

These limits do not permit false deletion completion or access to another owner's assets. They remain explicit future release or operational gates, not hidden evidence of production readiness.
